# Visual comparison rubric

Scores mean 1 broken/absent, 2 major shortcomings, 3 needs revision, 4 strong and fit for purpose, 5 exceptional. Score the visual role of each state, not whether every state is a geographic map.

- Architecture: coherent, recognizable, detailed structures rather than simplified blocks. Neighborhood context may use smaller structures but its landmarks must retain a clear visual presence.
- Landscape: ground, planting and spatial context support the composition. In a close-up, judge the illustrated model's landscaping, contact with its ground, and neighborhood context thumbnail; an intentional studio backdrop does not by itself fail.
- Lighting: consistent, dimensional lighting and grounded shadows.
- Composition: clear visual hierarchy, cohesive scene, unclipped key forms, useful balance of image and controls.
- Readability: labels, hierarchy and content can be read at the actual tested width, without overlap or offscreen actions.
- Usefulness: a visible path to actual nested places and source-backed visitor information; opening must reveal useful choices.
- Geographic integrity: full-area extent in neighborhood states, separate source coordinates from illustration registration, no invented exact locations for unknown places. Close-ups need recognizable models, explicit non-floorplan caveats and a coherent return to the full neighborhood, not surveyed room geometry.

A pass is a review judgment, not proof of surveyed accuracy, inventory completeness beyond the saved records, owner design approval or launch permission. Do not redefine criteria after seeing a failure merely to force a pass. Preserve independent review notes and explain coordinator disagreements.
